---
title: Desktop tour
description: The panel, the overview, workspaces, hot corners, and how to find your way around.
---

The Singularity desktop keeps the screen quiet. Two things are always present:
a thin panel at the top and a dock at the bottom. Everything else appears when
you ask for it.

## The panel

The panel runs along the top edge. On the left you will find the active app and
a way into the overview. The center holds the [global menu](/docs/global-menu/),
the menus for the app you are using. The right side has a compact cluster for
network, sound and battery, a clock, and notifications. The clock can show
12-hour or 24-hour time.

The panel stays out of the way. It can run flat, taking on a solid background,
and it does so on its own when a window is maximized, then goes transparent again
when the screen is clear. It also hides entirely for fullscreen windows. If you
prefer one less bar, the panel can fuse into the dock and become a single
element. See [the dock](/docs/dock/).

## The overview and the launcher

Press Super and Space to open the overview: your apps and running windows in one
place, with search at the top. The search there is the deep one, finding apps,
your files, and even the answer to a quick calculation. The launcher itself comes
in two styles, a fullscreen grid or a compact menu, so you can choose how much it
takes over the screen.

For launching by name without the overview, there is [Spotlight](/docs/spotlight/).

## Workspaces

Press Super and W for the workspace overview, a grid of your workspaces with the
windows on each. Workspaces can be dynamic, created and removed as you need them,
or a fixed number you set yourself.

## Windows

Windows get rounded corners by default, which drop away when a window is
maximized or tiled. Optional tiling arranges windows into a layout, with Super
and R to re-tile, though tiling is still experimental and off by default; see
[Developer mode](/docs/developer-mode/). The app switcher can show as a vertical
list or a grid of icons.

## Hot corners

Pushing the pointer into a screen corner triggers an action. Each of the four
corners can be set to open the workspaces, open the overview, open settings, or
do nothing. By default the top-left opens the workspaces and the top-right opens
settings.

## Multiple monitors

The panel and the dock can each appear on every monitor or just one, and you can
choose which monitor hosts the shell's windows when you have more than one.
